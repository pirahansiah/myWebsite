Embedded Edge AI & Computer Vision Consultant in Germany

I am Dr. Farshid Pirahansiah: an independent consultant for embedded edge AI and computer vision, working with engineering teams in Germany and across Europe — remotely the whole working week, and on site for the milestones that need a whiteboard. 12+ years, 21 publications, 3 patents, and a working rule: the model only counts once it runs on the device in front of the customer.

The teams I work with are not looking for a general machine-learning lecture. They already have a model, a board, a deadline, and something that is too slow, too hot, or not reproducible on the hardware they have to ship. Pain points I am usually called for

These are the situations that bring teams to me. Each one is a real engineering problem I have written up on this site — the links go to the notes, so you can judge the depth before we ever talk.

"Our detector runs at 4 FPS on the Jetson we already bought"

An FP32 PyTorch model on an embedded device is the classic case. The work: build a TensorRT engine for the exact target, calibrate INT8 on your own images, move pre-processing onto the GPU so the CPU stops being the bottleneck, and batch what can be batched. See CUDA development on Windows and How PyCUDA compiles and runs C kernels for the toolchain side, and the CV, DL & ML optimization guide for the method-by-method approach.

"It works on the workstation and falls apart on the device"

Memory ceilings, thermal throttling and a different operator set are three different problems that look alike. The work: measure the real budget on the device, profile where the frames are actually lost, and shrink the model to fit the memory you have. The local-LLM-on-a-weak-machine write-up is the same method applied to a language model on an 8 GB machine.

"We have cameras, but the system does not see reliably"

Calibration, sync and geometry before networks: multi-camera rigs where the frames do not line up, optical flow that breaks under fast motion or changing light, 3D reconstruction that drifts. See 3D vision & real-time multi-camera systems, Optical flow: challenges and solutions and the cabling notes for multi-camera capture.

"We need a model on the device, without a cloud and without a GPU"

Small-model work for edge devices: choosing an architecture that fits, quantizing it, and running it locally — from a 1-bit 4B language model on an old laptop (notes) to image generation on 8 GB, plus the ESP32-C3 board work where the whole budget is a few hundred kilobytes.

How an engagement usually works

  1. A call about the actual bottleneck — the model, the hardware, the deadline and the accuracy that is non-negotiable. No slide deck, no discovery phase invoice.
  2. A short technical audit — profiling on your target device, then a written finding: what is slow, what is fixable, what will not fit, and where the risk sits.
  3. Implementation together — I work inside your repository and review flow, or hand your engineers a working reference they can extend. C++, Python and CUDA, in your toolchain and your CI.
  4. Hand-over — the pipelines, the profiling script, and the reasoning, documented so the next engineer (or the next model) can reproduce it.
